## Frame.io: keyboard shortcuts, hover frame preview, comments on ranges

### Takeaway
Frame.io's current (V4) shortcut list confirms the NLE grammar: Space/K, J/L at multiple speeds, Shift+J/L for fine steps, arrows for ±1 frame, Shift+arrows for ±10 frames, I/O, Shift+I/O, R for a range, C for a comment. It also adds an in-app "?" shortcut panel and remappable shortcuts. One correction: V4's "anchored comments" are pinned to a *pixel location* on the frame, not to a time range. The "redesigned player" details that the earlier notes dated "probably 2025" are actually from February 2018.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED** V4 hover preview is frame-accurate: "We've also added frame-accurate seeking to the player, which helps you find the exact moment you're looking for by hovering over the timeline and seeing a preview of each frame as you move along." The beta post also says "frame-accurate hover scrubbing" and "high-resolution preview during scrubbing" — [Frame.io blog, V4 Player and Commenting, 2024-05-28](https://blog.frame.io/2024/05/28/frame-io-v4-features-player-and-commenting/); [Frame.io blog, V4 beta, 2024-04-09](https://blog.frame.io/2024/04/09/frame-io-version-4-beta-is-here/)
- **CORRECTED** The earlier notes said V4 comments are "anchored to a point on video". The source means a *spatial* point: "anchor your comments directly to any point or pixel in your video, image, or design-frame. Simply tap anywhere on the frame". "Anchored comments are really easy to find for new users, which is helpful for reviewers who might be unfamiliar with the concept of time-stamped comments." So anchored comments are pixel pins on top of time-stamped comments, not range comments — [V4 beta post](https://blog.frame.io/2024/04/09/frame-io-version-4-beta-is-here/); [V4 player post](https://blog.frame.io/2024/05/28/frame-io-v4-features-player-and-commenting/)
- **NEW (V4 player post)** Controls sit in one fixed place for every media type: "these tools are pinned in the exact same spot in the bottom of the player, which minimizes visual distraction and lets the content shine." Keyboard focus is managed: "When you submit a comment, focus immediately shifts back to the player, so you can keep using my keyboard to control playback… When you start typing again, the player is immediately paused." There is also comment filtering and a link to every comment — [Frame.io blog 2024-05-28](https://blog.frame.io/2024/05/28/frame-io-v4-features-player-and-commenting/)
- **CORRECTED** The "redesigned player" features (highest resolution by default, 1:1 frame guides, dedicated audio player, new URL structure, "File metadata is clearly surfaced on top of the comments", "Robust and smooth performance for scrolling through hundreds of comments") come from a post "Posted by Emery Wells, February 2, 2018 (Updated December 19, 2023)". They are not V4 or 2025 features — [blog.frame.io/?p=4451](https://blog.frame.io/?p=4451)
- **CONFIRMED** Current V4 shortcuts: "Mark in point: I", "Mark out point: O", "Go to in point: Shift + I", "Go to out point: Shift + O", "Mark range: R", "Add a comment: C", "Reply to comment: Shift + R", "1 frame back: Left Arrow", "10 frames back: Shift + Left Arrow" — [Frame.io V4 Help, Keyboard shortcuts (updated 2026-09-25)](https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105337-keyboard-shortcuts)
- **NEW (V4 shortcuts)** "Play / pause: ␣ or K", "Play reverse multiple speeds: J", "Play forward multiple speeds: L", "Play incremental reverse multiple speeds: Shift + J", "Play incremental forward multiple speeds: Shift + L", "Play loop: ⌃ + L", "Mute: M", "Fullscreen: F", "Cmd + Scroll: Fluid zoom in and out", "Guides: G", "Add Annotation: P" — [same page](https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105337-keyboard-shortcuts)
- **NEW (closes the discoverability gap)** Frame.io has in-app shortcut help: "A full list of keyboard shortcuts can be found in-app… Type "?" … to open the panel." Shortcuts can be remapped: "Custom Keyboard Shortcuts allow you to remap any Frame.io keyboard shortcuts… You can also disable existing shortcuts" — [same page](https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105337-keyboard-shortcuts)
- **CONFIRMED** Legacy (V3) player: "C: Move the cursor into the Comment box and pause the clip", "I/O: In and Out points of a range-based comment", "Tab: Move cursor out of the Comment box". It also gives speed steps: "J to rewind 1x, 2x, 4x, and 8x… L to fast forward 1x, 2x, 4x, and 8x" and "Shift + Drag: Precision scrubbing" — [Frame.io Help, Keyboard shortcuts (Legacy)](https://help.frame.io/en/articles/7785-keyboard-shortcuts-legacy)
- **CONFIRMED + detail** "JKL shortcuts follow standard NLE behavior: (J) to rewind 2x,4x,8x… (L) fast forward 2x,4x,8x". The player shows the speed: "A visual signifier of your current speed will be displayed on your video each time you use a shortcut". The speed slider steps "by 0.05x iterations, all the way up to 1.75x… down to 0.25x". The FAQ notes that the last-used rate carries over to the next video — [Frame.io Help, Player page features (updated 2026-09-09)](https://support.frame.io/en/articles/7786-player-page-features)
- **CONFIRMED** Frame.io panel in Premiere Pro (beta) 25.6, July 2025: "Comments in Frame.io are now tied to markers in your sequence, rather than the original timestamp. That means you can finally edit your sequence without losing track of what part of your sequence a comment was about." — [Adobe Community announcement, 2025-07-24](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-732/now-in-beta-all-new-frame-io-panel-311901)

### Inferences
- DashLens can copy the Frame.io V4 key map exactly. There is now a primary source for the "?" overlay and remappable keys, so these are no longer only an inference.
- Show the current speed on screen whenever J/L changes it. Frame.io does this as standard.
- Return keyboard focus to the player after a note or flag is saved, and pause while the user types, as V4 does.
- Range notes (I/O then C) are documented in the legacy player. In V4, "Mark range: R" exists, but no V4 page I read says outright that a comment can cover a range.

### Gaps
- No V4 help page read this session describes range comments, or comment markers ("comment heads") on the timeline, in words. Their styling is still unconfirmed.
- No Frame.io page gives grey values or design tokens.

## Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro: JKL speed steps, timeline zoom, multicam number keys

### Takeaway
Apple's Final Cut Pro guide is the clearest primary source. JKL runs up to 32x, each extra press doubles the speed, and holding K while pressing J or L steps one frame. Zoom is Cmd+ / Cmd− with Shift+Z to fit. Keys 1–9 cut or switch to multicam angles. An official Resolve shortcut PDF (actually the Resolve 11 guide) confirms J/K/L, I/O, Shift+I/O, Shift+Z, Cmd= / Cmd−. Adobe's own help pages could not be read, and the Premiere items rest on Adobe Community and ProVideo Coalition.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED (FCP, primary)** "You can use the J, K, and L keys on your keyboard to play a project or clip at up to 32x normal speed." "Double the current playback speed: Press L or J twice." "Move the playhead one frame at a time: Hold down the K key, and press J or L." — [Apple, Final Cut Pro User Guide: Play media](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/play-media-ver90ba4ef0/mac). This replaces the low-authority Tella source in the earlier notes.
- **CONFIRMED (FCP shortcut table)** "Play forward (press L multiple times to increase the playback speed) — L"; "Play in reverse (press J multiple times…) — J"; "Stop — K"; "Set Range Start — I"; "Set Range End — O"; "Go to Range Start — Shift-I"; "Go to Range End — Shift-O" — [Apple, Keyboard shortcuts in Final Cut Pro for Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver90ba5929/mac)
- **NEW / closes the zoom gap (FCP)** "Zoom In — Command-Plus Sign (+)", "Zoom Out — Command-Minus Sign (–)", "Zoom the contents to fit the size of the browser, viewer, or timeline — Zoom to Fit — Shift-Z" — [same page](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver90ba5929/mac)
- **NEW (FCP multicam)** "Cut and switch the multicam clip to angle 1 of the current bank — Cut and Switch to Viewer Angle 1 — 1" (and so on to 9). Previous/next angle uses arrow chords, for example "Previous Angle — Control-Shift-Left Arrow" — [same page](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver90ba5929/mac)
- **CORRECTED / version note (Resolve)** The earlier notes cited a third-party Pixflow guide. A Blackmagic PDF at the URL named "DaVinci-Resolve-15-Keyboard-Shortcuts.pdf" is headed "GUIDE - DAVINCI RESOLVE 11 MENUS & KEYBOARD SHORTCUTS". It lists "Play Forward L", "Play Reverse J", "Stop K", "Step One Frame Reverse Left Arrow", "Mark In I", "Mark Out O", "Go to In Shift I", "Go to Out Shift O", "Zoom to Fit Shift Z", "Zoom In Command Equals", "Zoom Out Command Hyphen", "Trim/Step Multiframe Left Shift Comma" — [Blackmagic Design PDF](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci-Resolve-15-Keyboard-Shortcuts.pdf). This is an old version. Current Resolve 19/20 shortcut PDFs could not be found at the guessed URLs (301 redirects).
- **CORRECTED (Premiere JKL thread)** The earlier notes said "in recent Premiere versions J/K/L are mapped to shuttle and some users ask for the older fixed-speed behaviour". The thread is from June 2018. The user had changed a setting by accident, and the reply says "Shuttle operation does not appear to have changed… I confirmed for CS6", then describes the behaviour: "L speeds up… If in faster than normal forward playback, the J key… continues forward play one step slower… J reverses playback only if playback was at normal speed." — [Adobe Community, 2018-06-06](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/j-k-l-keys-are-assigned-to-shuttle-but-used-to-be-playback-speed-how-do-i-change-it-back/m-p/9938004)
- **CONFIRMED (Premiere Shift+J/L)** "holding down the Shift key and tapping the L key started playing back footage starting at one frame per second. Tapping Shift + L multiple times slowly ratcheted up the speed… Adobe calls it Shuttle Slow Right and Shuttle Slow Left." — [ProVideo Coalition, 2024-11-05](https://www.provideocoalition.com/tool-tip-tuesday-for-adobe-premiere-pro-add-the-shift-key-to-step-playback/)
- **CONFIRMED (Premiere multicam, community answer, 2016)** "To toggle cameras with a shortcut you can hit the number keys, 1-9, and to cut to the cameras while playing back you can add the Ctrl modifier to those number keys." — [Adobe Community, 2016-08-23](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/multi-camera-editing-switching-cameras/td-p/8493110). The earlier notes' phrase "each choice made while playing is recorded as an edit" is not in this answer.
- **NOT FOUND** Adobe's official Premiere default-shortcut table (helpx.adobe.com could not be reached). A search summary of Adobe helpx pages reports "J Shuttle Left, L Shuttle Right, Shift+J/L Shuttle Slow" and "Zoom In =" for Premiere. That is search-summary evidence only — [helpx default shortcuts (unread)](https://helpx.adobe.com/au/premiere-pro/using/default-keyboard-shortcuts.chromeless.html)
- **NOT FOUND** The Blackmagic Forum threads on Resolve multicam angle keys failing in 1x1 view (p=420091, p=547295) returned no content. Resolve's angle-switching shortcuts are not confirmed from a primary source.
- **NOT RE-CHECKED** The Storyblocks claim that Resolve ships Premiere/FCP/Avid key presets, the Duke QuickTime page, and the Avid MediaCentral page.

### Inferences
- A DashLens shortcut set of J/K/L with doubling speeds (Frame.io: 2x/4x/8x; FCP: up to 32x), K+J/K+L for single frames, I/O and Shift+I/O, Cmd/Ctrl +/− and Shift+Z to zoom the timeline, and 1–9 for camera angles now matches documented FCP, Frame.io and Resolve defaults.
- Frame.io caps J/L at 8x, and dashcam drives are long, so DashLens could go higher (16x/32x, as FCP does) when scanning a whole trip.

### Gaps
- Official Premiere Pro shortcut table (helpx) and current Resolve 19/20 shortcut documents were not read.
- Resolve multicam angle keys were not confirmed.

## YouTube keyboard shortcuts

### Takeaway
Every YouTube claim in the earlier notes is confirmed on the official help page. The page also shows two discoverability patterns DashLens can copy: Shift+? opens a shortcut list, and the key appears in each button's tooltip.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED** "j — Seek backward 10 seconds in player." "l — Seek forward 10 seconds in player." "Left/Right arrow on the seek bar — Seek backward/forward 5 seconds." "Numbers 1 to 9 — Seek to the 10% to 90% of the video." "Number 0 — Seek to the beginning of the video." — [YouTube Help, Keyboard shortcuts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7631406?hl=en)
- **CONFIRMED** "'.' While the video is paused, skip to the next frame." "',' While the video is paused, go back to the previous frame." — [same](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7631406?hl=en)
- **CONFIRMED** "If you're using the new computer experience, you must click the video player before using keyboard shortcuts." — [same](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7631406?hl=en)
- **NEW** "You can also enter SHIFT+? on your keyboard. When you mouse over certain player buttons, you'll see the relevant keyboard shortcut… 'Full screen (f)'". Also "'>' Speed up the video playback rate", "'<' Slow down", "k — Pause/Play", "m — Mute", "f — full screen", "c — captions", "Ctrl + → … Go to the next chapter" — [same](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7631406?hl=en)

### Inferences
- The J/L conflict holds: YouTube jumps ±10 s, while NLEs and Frame.io shuttle. Showing the speed on screen (as Frame.io does) is the documented way to avoid confusing YouTube users.
- Tooltips in the form "Play (K)" and a "?" overlay now have two primary sources (YouTube and Frame.io V4).

### Gaps
- None for this question.

## Dashcam viewers: BlackVue, Dashcam Viewer, Tesla, Viofo, Nextbase, Garmin, Thinkware, Rivian

### Takeaway
The shared layout (video, map with speed and coordinates, G-sensor graph, file-type filter) is confirmed for BlackVue, Thinkware, Viofo and Nextbase. Several earlier details need correcting. BlackVue's manual lists impact, overspeed, parking motion and manual triggers, not "hard braking/geofence". Dashcam Viewer's 2018 manual limits free users to 2 *geotags*, while the vendor's 2020 forum post limits them to 2 *videos*. Tesla's pinch-to-zoom came with the 2025 Holiday Update, not 2025.20. The biggest new finding is that Tesla now publishes its own **open-source, browser-only dashcam viewer ("SEI Explorer")** that shows frame-synced telemetry. That is a direct reference point and competitor for DashLens.

### Cited Findings
**Tesla**
- **CONFIRMED + major new detail** "With the "2025 Holiday Update" (firmware version 2025.44.25)… this data can be read and visualized with a new, web-based open-source tool. The telemetry data is embedded as SEI… directly into the H.264/H.265 video stream and is frame-synchronized with the footage". Fields include "speed in meters per second, steering wheel angle, accelerator pedal position… brake status, turn signal activity… Autopilot status: OFF, AUTOSTEER, FSD, or TACC… GPS coordinates… linear acceleration values on three axes". "The dashcam viewer runs entirely locally in the browser… Users can drag and drop MP4 files into the tool". It needs HW3 or HW4 — [heise online](https://heise.de/-11112252)
- **CONFIRMED (Tesla's own page)** "Tools for viewing and extracting metadata from supported Tesla Dashcam videos. SEI Explorer — view dashcam footage with SEI metadata displayed alongside video playback. Python CLI… Protobuf schema". "Only clips recorded on Tesla firmware 2025.44.25 or later and HW3 or above contain SEI data. If car is parked, SEI data may not be present." — [teslamotors.github.io/dashcam](https://teslamotors.github.io/dashcam)
- **CONFIRMED** "Sentry Mode recordings while parked do not contain telemetry data, as the vehicle shuts down sensors to save energy. For the advanced dashcam functions in the Tesla app, users also need Premium Connectivity and at least app version 4.51.5." ## Evidence handling: SWGDE, NIST, UK portal/police, hashing, and whether any consumer viewer exports a hashed manifest

### Takeaway
Primary sources now back the earlier vendor-blog advice. NIST IR 8387 calls hashing best practice (SHA preferred, MD5/SHA-1 acceptable) and says hashes should be stored separately. SWGDE says files should be "organized, verified (e.g., validating checksums)", and that the original should be preserved, with stream copy or lossless export used when a derivative is needed. The UK NDSP wants "a copy of the original file in unedited, uncompressed format" and makes the submitter keep the original. No consumer dashcam viewer read this session (BlackVue, Dashcam Viewer, Tesla SEI Explorer, Thinkware, Nextbase, Viofo) mentions hashing or a manifest.

### Cited Findings
- **NEW (NIST, primary)** "It is best practice to hash digital images and other objects using a NIST approved hashing algorithm and the resulting hashes should be stored separately from the image or file in a secure location." "It is acceptable to use an older hashing algorithm (e.g., MD5 or SHA-1) if needed but newer algorithms are preferred." "By verifying hashes or digital signatures, it is accurate to state that the copies are identical to the original. The concept of "best evidence" is not applicable since the copy is identical to the original." It also says to keep more than one copy, and that block hashes can limit how much data is suspect — [NIST IR 8387, Digital Evidence Preservation (2022)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2022/NIST.IR.8387.pdf)
- **NEW (SWGDE canvassing)** "The files should be organized, verified (e.g., validating checksums) and protected from alteration. Documentation of the video files should include the date and time of transfer, as well as the location from which the files were collected." Anonymous submissions: "it would be advantageous to ascertain the original recording device's make and model" — [SWGDE Guidelines for Video Evidence Canvassing and Collection v1.0 (2021-01-14)](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2021-01-14-SWGDE-Guidelines-for-Video-Evidence-Canvassing-and-Collection_v1.0.pdf)
- **NEW (SWGDE authentication)** Questions examiners ask: "Is the file a camera-original file?" "Has the file been re-encoded?" It names "stream hashing" to check that a conversion did not alter the stream. "Metadata is susceptible to alteration without affecting the playback of the file." Public-portal submissions "should be evaluated for their originality" — [SWGDE Best Practices for Digital Video Authentication 23-V-001-1.2 (2024-03-07)](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024-03-07-SWGDE-Best-Practices-for-Digital-Video-Authentication-23-V-001-1.2.pdf)

## Video grading UI neutrality: neutral grey surrounds

### Takeaway
The 18% grey convention in Frame.io's guide is about **room walls**, not the software interface, so the earlier "for both the interface and the walls" was wrong. The standards (ITU-R BT.2035) set a D65-neutral background behind the picture at about 10% of reference white. That supports a neutral, untinted surround but gives no UI hex values. No Blackmagic primary document on UI greys was found.

### Cited Findings
- **CORRECTED** Frame.io's guide: "establish a neutral color environment… By painting the walls neutral gray, specifically 18% gray or middle gray… The reason this gray is necessary is that it keeps your perception of color constant." It also says lights should be "balanced to a 6500k color temperature" with "CRI… of at least 90". The interface is not mentioned — [Frame.io Workflow Guide, Color Correction Suites](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/color-correction-suites)
- **NEW (standard)** "Chromaticity of background: D65"; "Ratio of luminance of background behind picture to peak luminance of picture: ≈ 10% ±2% of monitor reference white value"; "The colour of the display background should be the same as the reference white point; for the remaining room surfaces dark matte surfaces should be used. The objective is to minimize stray light on the display screen." — [ITU-R BT.2035-0 (2013)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.2035-0-201307-I!!PDF-E.pdf)
- **NOT FOUND** Envato Tuts+ (403) and the KDE Discuss thread (page returned no readable text) could not be checked. A search summary says the KDE thread refers to a DaVinci Resolve setting "Use Gray Background" and that a default dark theme has "a distinct blue tint". This is unverified — [KDE Discuss](https://discuss.kde.org/t/built-in-gray-theme/45385)
- **NOT RE-CHECKED** The Adobe Community light-UI requests.
- **NOT FOUND** Any Blackmagic Design document stating the UI grey values or the rationale behind them.

### Inferences
- Keep the DashLens video surround neutral (zero chroma) and fairly dark but not black. The ~10%-of-white background in BT.2035 is a reasonable reference for the area right around the video. This is an inference: BT.2035 covers a room and display setup, not app chrome.
- Keep saturated colour for markers and state, as before.
