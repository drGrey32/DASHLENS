# Put DashLens on the desktop grid

DashLens 1.6.1 passes the hard desktop rules in most places, but it misses the shared desktop grid. Its 26, 30 and 36 px controls all meet the WCAG 2.2 AA floor of 24 × 24 CSS px. None of them is a size that any of the six desktop design systems checked uses. Those systems agree on **24 / 32 / 40 px** controls, a **4 px spacing grid** with about ten steps, body text at **14 px** with 12 px as the smallest step, and weights of **400 to 600**. Three items fail outright. The 80 × 16 px volume slider is under 24 px tall. It sits in the middle of a horizontal toolbar, where the WAI-ARIA toolbar pattern says a slider must not go. Text renders at 700 and 900, but the embedded IBM Plex faces stop at 600 and DashLens's own DESIGN.md allows only 400–600. Toasts close after 2.4–2.8 s, which is under every documented minimum (5 s in Carbon and Spectrum, 8 s in Atlassian). The spacing drift is the largest: 12 gap values and 49 padding values. Six of those gap values (5, 7, 10, 11, 14, 18) are not steps in any system read. For the whole app, the best reference is **Frigate's History view**. It has a main player, synced camera previews, a side panel with Timeline, Events and Detail, and an export dialog. For each element, a specialist tool is better: Frame.io for transport and keyboard help, the DaVinci Resolve Cut page for the long-drive timeline, Final Cut Pro for the multicam grid, Strava for telemetry lanes, Dashcam Viewer for the map, VS Code for settings, Primer and Carbon for dialogs, and Atlassian, Spectrum and GOV.UK for notifications. All design-system numbers below come from the systems' own published packages or pages, read on 2026-11-26. The points that remain unverified are listed near the end.

## Six design systems agree on 24, 32 and 40 px controls

### WCAG 2.2 sets the desktop floor at 24 px, and a slider is one target

The binding AA rule for a pointer-driven desktop app is **SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)**. "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels". A smaller target passes only through exceptions: spacing, an equivalent control, inline text, user-agent control, or essential presentation. The spacing test centres a 24 px circle on each undersized target. No circle may touch another target or another circle ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum)). The W3C chose 24 px on purpose for "all pointer input modalities – not just touch, but also mouse, trackpad, stylus". It still advises authors to "at least meet the minimum size requirement … regardless of spacing" ([Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). The 44 × 44 px figure belongs to **SC 2.5.5, which is AAA** and has no spacing exception ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-enhanced)). It is a touch goal, not a desktop minimum. Note 1 of 2.5.8 is decisive for DashLens. Sliders and other controls that pick a value by position "are considered one target" ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum)). So the whole volume slider must be 24 px tall. Its 80 × 16 px box passes today only if nothing else is inside its 24 px circles. Padding the box to 24 px is simpler and safer.

Reflow also applies to a desktop-only app. SC 1.4.10 asks for no two-way scrolling at **320 CSS px wide**, which "is equivalent to a starting viewport width of 1280 CSS pixels wide at 400% zoom". Video, maps, data tables and "interfaces where it is necessary to keep toolbars in view" are exempt ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#reflow)). The W3C's passing example is a photo editor whose "panels and dialogs are resizable and can be made to fit within a 320px wide viewport" ([Understanding 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)). For DashLens, the video stage, map and timeline are exempt. The settings drawer, dialogs, event list and incident summary are not. They must fit 320 px without horizontal scrolling. At 200 % zoom a 1280 px laptop gives only 640 CSS px, and a 1920 px monitor gives 960, so the layout must work in that range too ([Understanding 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)).

### The desktop default control is 32 px everywhere except Carbon

The control ladders were read from source code. **Primer** has tokens of 24, 28, 32, 40 and 48 px, and its Button is 32 px by default with 14 px text ([Primer size.css](https://unpkg.com/@primer/primitives@11.10.0/dist/css/functional/size/size.css); [Primer ButtonBase](https://unpkg.com/@primer/react@38.47.0/dist/Button/ButtonBase-c8dab214.css)). **Fluent 2** buttons come out at 24, 32 and 40 px from padding plus line height, with 12, 14 and 16 px text. Fluent inputs are set directly to `small: '24px', medium: '32px', large: '40px'` ([Fluent Button source](https://unpkg.com/@fluentui/react-button@9.11.4/lib/components/Button/useButtonStyles.styles.raw.js); [Fluent Input source](https://unpkg.com/@fluentui/react-input@9.8.10/lib/components/Input/useInputStyles.styles.raw.js)). **Spectrum** gives each size a desktop and a mobile value: 24/30, **32/40** and 40/50 px ([Spectrum tokens](https://unpkg.com/@adobe/spectrum-tokens@15.9.1/src/layout-component.json)). **Atlassian** buttons are 32 px, and 24 px in the compact variant "for tables" ([Atlassian Button source](https://unpkg.com/@atlaskit/button@25.8.3/dist/esm/new-button/variants/shared/button-base.js)). **Carbon** has the same 24/32/40/48 ladder, but its Button defaults to 48 px and its inputs to 40 px ([Carbon _button.scss](https://unpkg.com/@carbon/styles@1.120.0/scss/components/button/_button.scss)). Carbon also says, "We do not recommend mixing different button sizes in button groups" ([Carbon Button style](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/button/style/)). **Apple** gives macOS a 28 × 28 pt default and a 20 × 20 pt minimum control size ([Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). Its Buttons page also says a button "needs a hit region of at least 44x44 pt" as "a general rule" ([Apple HIG Buttons JSON](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons.json)). The two Apple pages conflict. The WCAG 24 px floor with a 32 px default sits between them.

The sizes pair with text in a fixed way. A 24 px control holds 12 px text. A 32 px control holds 14 px text. Primer's inline padding is 8, 12 or 16 px (condensed, normal, spacious), its block padding is 4 px in small and 6 px in medium controls, and its icon-to-label gap is 4 px in small and 8 px in medium controls ([Primer size.css](https://unpkg.com/@primer/primitives@11.10.0/dist/css/functional/size/size.css)). Primer gives one more warning. Its small button can fall under 24 px because of **width**, not height: "a single letter "i" would result in a Button width of 21.5 CSS pixels" ([Primer Button accessibility](https://primer.style/product/components/button/accessibility/)). A square control therefore needs `min-width` equal to its height. GNOME adds two rules that suit a toolbar: "Do not use more than one or two different widths of button in the same window", and "Each view should only ever include a single button using either the suggested or destructive styles" ([GNOME HIG Buttons](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/patterns/controls/buttons.html)). Apple says to "Use style — not size — to visually distinguish the preferred choice" ([Apple HIG Buttons JSON](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons.json)). So the Play button stays 32 px like its neighbours and stands out by fill, not by size.

### Sliders draw a thin track and keep a thick hit box

All three systems that ship a slider draw a thin track with a much larger thumb. **Carbon** uses a 2 px track and a 14 px thumb that scales to about 20 px on hover, focus and drag, with a 200 px minimum width ([Carbon slider SCSS](https://unpkg.com/@carbon/styles@1.120.0/scss/components/slider/_slider.scss)). **Fluent 2** small uses a 2 px rail, a 16 px thumb and a 24 px minimum height. Fluent medium uses a 4 px rail, a 20 px thumb and a 32 px minimum height. The horizontal minimum width is **120 px** ([Fluent slider source](https://unpkg.com/@fluentui/react-slider@9.6.9/lib/components/Slider/useSliderStyles.styles.raw.js)). The only current **Spectrum** track token is 4 px for medium. Its old 2 px track and 16 px handle tokens are now deprecated ([Spectrum slider tokens](https://unpkg.com/@adobe/spectrum-tokens@15.9.1/src/slider.json)). Fluent's usage page names the DashLens case: "Sliders can take immediate effect, like a volume control". It also says, "Use sliders with a label, even if that label is hidden", and that a value tooltip "must be perceivable to people using screen readers" ([Fluent Slider usage](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/components/web/react/core/slider/usage)). Carbon's keyboard rule is one step per arrow press and ten steps with Shift ([Carbon Slider usage](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/slider/usage/)). The DashLens slider should therefore be a **120 × 24 px** element with a **4 px** drawn track and a **16 px** thumb that grows to **20 px** on hover, focus and drag. The same rule applies to the timeline scrubber. A 2–4 px drawn line sits inside a hit strip at least 24 px tall.

### A toolbar is one Tab stop, and its slider goes last

The WAI-ARIA toolbar pattern is strict. "The keyboard tab sequence includes one stop for the toolbar and arrow keys move focus among the controls in the toolbar." In a horizontal toolbar, authors should "Avoid including controls whose operation requires the pair of arrow keys used for toolbar navigation. If unavoidable, include only one such control and make it the last element" ([APG Toolbar Pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/toolbar/)). The APG also recommends "a documented shortcut key for moving focus" to a toolbar where quick access matters. DashLens 1.6.1 marks its playback bar `role="toolbar"` but does not move focus with the arrow keys (index.html, line 414). The volume slider sits in the middle, followed by the audio-source select, the skip button and the zoom buttons (lines 414–440). There is also a real conflict. DashLens uses Left and Right for frame steps across the whole page. If arrows move focus inside the toolbar, a mouse user who clicks Play and then presses Right moves focus instead of stepping a frame. Frame.io solves a similar case: "When you submit a comment, focus immediately shifts back to the player" ([Frame.io blog](https://blog.frame.io/2024/05/28/frame-io-v4-features-player-and-commenting/)). The fix is to implement the roving tabindex, put the volume slider last, and return focus to the player after a pointer click on a transport button. The last step is this report's inference, not a sourced rule.

### Focus and tooltips have exact numbers now

The focus rules are AA 2.4.7, "a mode of operation where the keyboard focus indicator is visible", and AA 2.4.11, under which a focused control must not be "entirely hidden due to author-created content" ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-visible); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum)). Sticky footers and non-modal dialogs are the typical cause, and CSS `scroll-padding` is the W3C technique against it ([Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). AAA 2.4.13 asks for an indicator at least as large as "a 2 CSS pixel thick perimeter" with **3:1 between focused and unfocused pixels**. The W3C says "the simplest way to meet the size requirement is to use a focus indicator which is a solid 2 CSS pixel thick perimeter" ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-appearance); [Understanding 2.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html)). DashLens's 2 px orange outline meets this. Its offsets vary (−2, 1 and 2 px), so it needs one rule.

Tooltip show delays differ widely between the systems, and the values come from their source code. **Primer** waits 50 ms ("short", the default), with 400 ms and 1200 ms options. It shows on focus only for `:focus-visible` and sets `max-width:250px` ([Primer Tooltip source](https://unpkg.com/@primer/react@38.47.0/dist/TooltipV2/Tooltip.js)). **Carbon** waits 100 ms and closes after 300 ms, or 100 ms on icon buttons ([Carbon Tooltip source](https://unpkg.com/@carbon/react@1.121.0/es/components/Tooltip/Tooltip.js); [Carbon IconButton source](https://unpkg.com/@carbon/react@1.121.0/es/components/IconButton/index.js)). **Fluent 2** waits 250 ms and closes after 250 ms. It shows at once "if another tooltip is already visible", hides on blur and on Escape, and places the tip 4 px from its target ([Fluent Tooltip source](https://unpkg.com/@fluentui/react-tooltip@9.10.9/lib/components/Tooltip/useTooltipBase.js); [Fluent Tooltip usage](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/components/web/react/core/tooltip/usage)). **React Spectrum** waits 1500 ms and keeps a 500 ms cooldown, with a global warm-up timer ([react-stately source](https://unpkg.com/react-stately@3.51.0/dist/private/tooltip/useTooltipTriggerState.mjs)). WCAG 1.4.13 requires custom tooltips to be dismissible, hoverable and persistent. Esc is the usual dismiss key ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#content-on-hover-or-focus)). Primer says to "Reserve Tooltips to visually surface the label for IconButtons" ([Primer Tooltip guidelines](https://primer.style/product/components/tooltip/guidelines/)). GNOME says that if one control in a container has a tooltip, all of them should ([GNOME HIG Tooltips](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/patterns/feedback/tooltips.html)). DashLens uses 41 native `title` attributes and no custom tooltip. WCAG counts `title` as user-agent content, so it is exempt from 1.4.13 ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#content-on-hover-or-focus)). But the author cannot set its delay. In common browsers it also does not appear on keyboard focus, which is this report's observation, not a sourced claim. Keyboard users therefore never see the shortcut hints that DESIGN.md §7 puts in tooltips.

### Switches act at once; checkboxes wait for Save

NN/g, Carbon, Primer and Fluent all say a switch must take effect immediately. NN/g says toggles "should not require the user to click Save or Submit" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/toggle-switch-guidelines/)). Primer says, "ToggleSwitches act as instant-save controls for boolean settings, while checkboxes are for selections requiring explicit save actions" ([Primer ToggleSwitch](https://primer.style/product/components/toggle-switch/guidelines/)). Apple says to keep switches out of toolbars and, in macOS, "don't replace a checkbox with a switch" ([Apple HIG Toggles JSON](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles.json)). Fluent's toolbar equivalent is the toggle button ([Fluent Button usage](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/components/web/react/core/button/usage)). In DashLens, live settings use switches, the Save-evidence options use checkboxes, and toolbar modes such as Mute and Follow use toggle buttons.

## Hierarchy comes from size, weight and lightness, never from 900

### Desktop body text is 14 px, and the systems step 12, 14, 16, 20

Every major desktop system sets default UI text at 13–14 px. Carbon, Fluent, Primer, Atlassian and Spectrum desktop all use **14 px** body. Apple's macOS body is 13 pt ([@carbon/type](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@carbon/type); [@fluentui/tokens](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fluentui/tokens); [@primer/primitives](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@primer/primitives); [@atlaskit/tokens](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@atlaskit/tokens); [Apple HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Spectrum shows the desktop and mobile split in one token. `font-size-100` is 14 px on desktop and 17 px on mobile ([@adobe/spectrum-tokens](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@adobe/spectrum-tokens)). The smallest step is 12 px everywhere. Primer limits its 12 px caption "for single-line scenarios, as the small sizing doesn't pass accessibility requirements" ([@primer/primitives](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@primer/primitives)). The common role steps are **12 → 14 → 16 → 20 → 24/28 → 32**. Carbon's raw scale does contain 18, Spectrum has 18 as `font-size-300`, Apple uses 15 pt for Title 3, and Geist has label-13 and label-18 ([Geist Typography](https://vercel.com/geist/typography)). No system, however, uses 13, 15 and 18 together as its UI roles. DashLens uses six sizes today: 12, 13, 15, 16, 18 and 30 px. Its 13 px body sits between Apple and everyone else. Its 15 and 18 px section and title steps are only one or two pixels from 14, 16 and 20.

This corrects the earlier verified report, which called DashLens's 12/13/15/18 tokens "the right scale". The wider sample here points to **12 / 14 / 16 / 20 px plus one display size**. The line heights are 16, 20, 24 and 28 px, all multiples of 4, as Carbon and Fluent use ([@carbon/type](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@carbon/type); [@fluentui/tokens](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fluentui/tokens)). The move from 13 to 14 px body costs about 8 % more width per label. That is the main risk of the change. It must be tested at 1280 px and at 200 % zoom before release.

### Pro systems stop at 600, and big numerals get lighter, not heavier

Carbon has only three weights, 300, 400 and 600, with no 700 at all. Its headings get lighter as they grow: 600 at 14–16 px, 400 at 20–32 px and 300 at 42 px and up ([@carbon/type](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@carbon/type)). Fluent uses 600 for every heading, Display included, and offers 700 only as a "Stronger" variant of small text ([@fluentui/tokens](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fluentui/tokens)). Primer stops at 600 and sets its 40 px display style at 500 ([@primer/primitives](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@primer/primitives)). Atlassian is the outlier, with one "bold" of 653 that it also uses for metric numbers of 16, 24 and 28 px ([@atlaskit/tokens](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@atlaskit/tokens)). **No system read uses 900.** NN/g says hierarchy comes from "color and contrast, scale, and grouping", not from weight alone ([NN/g Visual Hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)).

The 700 and 900 in DashLens have a clear cause. The file has 34 `<b>` and `<strong>` elements and no rule that sets their weight. The browser default `bolder` turns 400 into 700 and 600 into 900. The speed readout sets `font:700 30px/1 var(--mono)` directly (index.html, line 96). The embedded faces cover only IBM Plex Sans 400–600 and IBM Plex Mono 400, 500 and 600 (lines 30–33). So the browser has no real 700 or 900 face. It either draws the 600 face or makes a synthetic bold. That last step is inference from CSS font matching and was not tested in a browser here. Either way, the rendered weight is not a design decision. The fix is one rule, `b, strong { font-weight: 600 }`, plus a readout at **28 px / 500** in Plex Mono with tabular figures. That matches Carbon's 28 px/400 heading-04 and Primer's 500 display style, and it uses a face that DashLens already embeds. The unit (km/h) goes beside it at 12 px in secondary text colour.

### Dark surfaces separate by lightness, with a ring on floating layers

Dark systems show elevation by making raised surfaces lighter. Radix steps about 4–8 lightness units (#111111, #191919, #222222, #2a2a2a), and Carbon steps 10–13 (#161616, #262626, #393939) ([@radix-ui/colors](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@radix-ui/colors); [@carbon/themes](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@carbon/themes)). Shadows are weak on dark backgrounds, so Primer and Atlassian add a 1 px light ring to floating shadows. Primer's dark `shadow-floating-small` starts with `0 0 0 1px #3d444d`. Atlassian's overlay shadow starts with `0 0 0 1px #BDBDBD1F`, about 12 % white ([@primer/primitives](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@primer/primitives); [@atlaskit/tokens](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@atlaskit/tokens)). DashLens has two shadow tokens (`--sh1`, `--sh2`) but four shadows in use. DESIGN.md §8 allows two. The two that stay should each carry the 1 px ring.

NN/g's squint test is the check for the whole screen. Blur a screenshot at 5–10 px and see what still stands out ([NN/g Visual Hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)). NN/g calls the F-pattern "the default pattern when there are no strong cues" ([NN/g F-pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)). In DashLens the video must win the blur test, then the playhead and the speed readout. A 900-weight label or a bright event chip must not win. Apple supports the permanent dark theme "In rare cases" for "immersive media viewing", because it "lets the UI recede" ([Apple HIG Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)). The amber accent creates one constraint. Carbon's warning (#f1c21b) and caution (#ff832b) are close to #ffb020 ([@carbon/themes](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@carbon/themes)). So a DashLens warning must use an icon and words, not an amber hue. NN/g adds that colour should mark category, not magnitude ([NN/g Dashboards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/)).

### One icon family, 16 px in rows and 20 px in the transport bar

Lucide's rules are "Strokes must be 2 pixels wide" on its 24 px canvas, and "Don't use thicker or thinner strokes, or mix stroke widths" ([Lucide](https://lucide.dev/guide/design/icon-design-guide)). Carbon uses a 16 px icon in buttons ([Carbon Button usage](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/button/usage/)). Fluent uses 20 px icons in its 24 and 32 px buttons ([Fluent Button source](https://unpkg.com/@fluentui/react-button@9.11.4/lib/components/Button/useButtonStyles.styles.raw.js)). For DashLens, the icon is 16 px in 24 px controls, lists and lanes, and 20 px in 32 px icon-only transport buttons. A scaled 24 px icon has thin strokes at 16 px, so one stroke width must be set for all icons. Matching it to Plex at 400–500 is a visual judgement, not a sourced rule.

## A 4 px grid with ten steps replaces 12 gaps and 49 paddings

### Every system shares 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32

The spacing scales were read from the packages. **Carbon** steps 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64 px and up ([Carbon _spacing.scss](https://unpkg.com/@carbon/layout@11.63.0/scss/generated/_spacing.scss)). **Atlassian** steps 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80 px ([@atlaskit/tokens](https://unpkg.com/@atlaskit/tokens@20.15.0/dist/esm/artifacts/token-default-values.js)). **Primer** steps 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48 px and up ([Primer base size](https://unpkg.com/@primer/primitives@11.10.0/dist/css/base/size/size.css)). **Spectrum** steps 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32 px and up ([Spectrum layout.json](https://unpkg.com/@adobe/spectrum-tokens@15.9.1/src/layout.json)). **Fluent** steps 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32 px and up. Fluent explains its odd steps: "The values 2, 6, and 10 account for extra padding in the Fluent icons" ([Fluent 2 Layout](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout)). **Polaris**, **Radix** and **Tailwind** follow the same 4 px base ([Polaris tokens](https://unpkg.com/@shopify/polaris-tokens@9.4.2/dist/css/styles.css); [Radix tokens](https://unpkg.com/@radix-ui/themes@3.3.0/tokens/base.css); [Tailwind theme](https://unpkg.com/tailwindcss@4.3.5/theme.css)). All eight share **4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32**. Seven add 2. Five add 6 and 20. **No system has 5, 7, 11, 14 or 18 as a spacing step**, and only Fluent has 10.

The reasons given are practical, not experimental. Elliot Dahl notes that "Scaling 5px by 1.5x will result in a half pixel offset" ([Dahl](https://medium.com/built-to-adapt/intro-to-the-8-point-grid-system-d2573cde8632)). Spec.fm says that removing "7 of every 8 spacing options" reduces fiddling and speeds up coding ([Spec.fm](https://spec.fm/specifics/8-pt-grid)). Carbon says "deviating from the spacing scales should be avoided whenever possible" ([Carbon Spacing](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/spacing/overview/)). Dahl adds a border rule. Keep the 8 px padding and let the 1 px border sit outside it, instead of using 7 px "subtract 1px" logic ([Dahl, Borders](https://medium.com/built-to-adapt/8-point-grid-borders-and-layouts-e91eb97f5091)). That rule removes most odd paddings such as 5, 7 and 11 px. No controlled study shows that a 4 px grid improves usability. The case rests on consistency and ease of build.

### Space inside a group must be smaller than space between groups

NN/g calls proximity "one of the most important grouping principles", strong enough to "overpower competing visual cues such as similarity of color or shape" ([NN/g Proximity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/)). Carbon says space can group information "without having to use lines or other graphical elements as a divider", and "Sections of a UI are allowed to be dense, but the whole page should not be crowded" ([Carbon Spacing](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/spacing/overview/)). Steve Schoger's advice is "Use fewer borders" and to space groups apart instead ([Refactoring UI](https://medium.com/refactoring-ui/7-practical-tips-for-cheating-at-design-40c736799886)). Atlassian's 2026 tokens split **gap** (2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24 px) from **inset** (padding of 2 to 48 px). Gap tokens say "Do not use for component insets" ([Atlassian spacing tokens](https://unpkg.com/@atlaskit/tokens@20.15.0/dist/esm/artifacts/tokens-raw/atlassian-spacing.js)). Every Layout gives the mechanism. Set space on the parent, because "margin is really a property of the relationship between two proximate elements" ([Every Layout, The Stack](https://every-layout.dev/layouts/stack/)). For DashLens, flex and grid `gap` on parents replaces most of the 49 padding and margin values. The ladder is 4–8 px inside a control, 8 px between related controls, 12–16 px for panel padding, 16–24 px between sections, and a shared 1 px edge or 8 px between app regions. Each level must be at least one step larger than the level inside it. No primary source states this "internal ≤ external" rule in those words. It follows from the proximity sources.

### Density changes control height, not the scale

The systems handle density by changing control height and inner padding, while the spacing scale stays the same. Primer offers condensed, normal and spacious padding. Spectrum changes only component heights between desktop and mobile. Radix scales everything from 90 % to 110 % with one factor ([Primer size.css](https://unpkg.com/@primer/primitives@11.10.0/dist/css/functional/size/size.css); [Spectrum layout.json](https://unpkg.com/@adobe/spectrum-tokens@15.9.1/src/layout.json); [Radix Spacing](https://www.radix-ui.com/themes/docs/theme/spacing)). A DashLens density option, if it comes, should change only `--ctl-sm` and `--ctl-md` and the inline padding.

### Breakpoints cluster at 1024, 1280, 1440 and 1920

Desktop breakpoints in the systems cluster around 1024–1056, 1280–1366, 1400–1584 and 1920 px ([Carbon breakpoints](https://unpkg.com/@carbon/layout@11.63.0/src/index.ts); [Fluent 2 Layout](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout); [Primer breakpoints](https://unpkg.com/@primer/primitives@11.10.0/dist/css/functional/size/breakpoints.css); [Tailwind theme](https://unpkg.com/tailwindcss@4.3.5/theme.css)). Text in drawers and dialogs should stay within 45–75 characters, and Every Layout's axiom is "the measure should never exceed 60ch" ([Every Layout, Axioms](https://every-layout.dev/rudiments/axioms/)). Carbon says video and data views should scale in size, not in the number of items ([Carbon 2x Grid](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/2x-grid/overview/)). Apple warns, "Avoid placing controls or critical information at the bottom of a window", because people drag windows partly off-screen ([Apple HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)). Video tools still pin transport at the bottom. Frame.io keeps its tools "pinned in the exact same spot in the bottom of the player" ([Frame.io blog](https://blog.frame.io/2024/05/28/frame-io-v4-features-player-and-commenting/)). DashLens can keep its bottom transport, because a browser tab is usually full-window, but no error or primary action should live only at the bottom edge. Vercel's alignment rules close the topic: "Every element aligns with something intentionally" and "Child radius ≤ parent radius" ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). The spacing notes found no primary source for "draw each border once". The earlier verified report did find it in Geist's colour guidance, so the rule stands ([Geist Colors](https://vercel.com/geist/colors)).

## Frigate is the whole-app model; a specialist leads each element

### Whole app: Frigate's layout with Dashcam Viewer's features

**Frigate History** (web, dark, free) is the closest documented single-page model. It has a main player and "a row of secondary previews". - **Toolbar:** a `role="toolbar"` has one Tab stop, and the arrow keys move focus inside it [APG]. A horizontal
  slider is the last control in the toolbar. After a pointer click on a transport button, the focus goes back to
  the player.
- **Focus ring:** 2 px solid accent, offset 2 px. Use -2 px only inside containers that clip [WCAG 2.4.13].
  Sticky parts never cover the focused control [WCAG 2.4.11].
- **Tooltips:** only on icon buttons, with the name and the key. Show after 400 ms; at once if another tooltip
  closed less than 500 ms ago; at once on keyboard focus. Hide on blur and Esc. The pointer can move onto the
  tooltip [WCAG 1.4.13]. Width at most 250 px, 4 px from the target. No tooltip during a drag.
- **Zoom:** drawers, dialogs, lists and the summary fit 320 CSS px wide without horizontal scroll [WCAG 1.4.10].
  Video, map and timeline are exempt.

## 8. Visual design (change)

- **Control heights:** 24, 32 and 40 px only. 24 px for timeline, lane, tile and chip controls (12 px text). 32 px
  for all other controls (14 px text). 40 px only for the start-screen primary action. Square buttons are as wide
  as they are tall. One height in each button group.
- **Spacing:** 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32 and 48 px only. Gaps use 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 or 24. Space inside a
  group is smaller than space between groups. Use `gap` on the parent, not margins on the children. Borders sit
  outside the padding.
- **Text sizes:** 12, 14, 16, 20 and 28 px only. 12 px for single lines only. 28 px for the speed readout only.
  Line heights are multiples of 4.
- **Weights:** 400, 500 and 600 only. `b` and `strong` are 600. The speed readout is 500.
- **Radii:** 4 px chips and lane marks; 6 px buttons, inputs and tooltips; 8 px tiles and cards; 12 px menus,
  drawers and dialogs. A child radius is never larger than its parent's.
- **Shadows:** two levels. Each starts with a 1 px ring at 12 % white, because a shadow alone does not show on
  the dark background.
- **Icons:** one family, one stroke width. 16 px in 24 px controls and lists, 20 px in 32 px icon-only buttons.
- **Warnings** use an icon and words, never an amber or orange colour, because orange is the accent.

## 11. Motion (add)

- **Toasts** stay 6 s. Hover or focus stops the timer. A toast with an action stays until the user closes it.

## 12. States (add)

- **Dialogs:** 320, 480 or 640 px wide, never wider than the window minus 32 px. Body padding 16 px. Close
  button 8 px from the edges. Footer on the right, primary button last, at most two buttons. First focus on the
  first input, or on the primary button. Esc closes only the top dialog.
- **Settings drawer:** 480 px. Changes apply at once. A changed setting has a 2 px accent bar and a Reset action.
  Switches for live settings; checkboxes where a Save button follows.

## 14. Notifications [R]

- **Toast:** only to confirm something the user did. Two lines at most. Never an error or a warning.
- **On-screen error:** for anything that went wrong. It stays until fixed or closed. Heading "There is a
  problem", one sentence of cause, one of fix. The focus moves to it.
- **No banner for input errors.** Show the error next to the input and in the error summary.

## Points that remain unverified

Several values could not be confirmed against primary sources, so this report does not rely on them. Apple's current macOS push-button heights are not on the HIG pages; only visionOS sizes are listed. The mapping of Spectrum's S/M/L button sizes to its 24/32/40 component heights was not read in the Spectrum CSS source. The Spectrum 2 slider handle diameter is not in the current tokens. YouTube and Vimeo scrubber sizes were not measured. Grafana's Ctrl+O shortcut is not on the current docs. Atlassian's 8 s auto-dismiss was read only in the package code, not on the docs page. IBM Plex's tabular-figure support was not checked in this session, although DashLens already uses tabular figures. That `title` tooltips do not show on keyboard focus, and that the browser synthesizes or clamps 700/900 for Plex, are this report's observations, not sourced claims. Material 3, Linear, Geist spacing tokens, Salesforce density, IINA's thumbnail seekbar, Garmin Connect's hover sync and UniFi's web timeline were not read in full. Sentry Studio rests on its README from the earlier pass. Cursor conventions (arrow versus hand on buttons) have no free primary source here, so DashLens must pick one rule itself. No usability study exists for any of the app patterns ranked above. The rankings are expert judgement from documentation, and the thresholds marked as choices (6 s toasts, 400 ms tooltips, the 1440 px inline drawer, the 0.05 volume step) are decisions inside the verified ranges, not values any one source prescribes.

## Conclusion

The desktop question turned out to be a question about drift, not about missing features. DashLens already does the hard things the references do: synced cameras, telemetry on the time axis, on-screen errors and an evidence export. What separates it from Primer, Fluent or Frigate is that their numbers come from a short closed list, and DashLens's numbers came from each feature as it was built. A 30 px button, a 5 px gap and a 900-weight label are each harmless alone. Together they are the main reason the app can still read as handmade next to a large-team product. The fix is mechanical and cheap. One CSS rule removes the 700 and 900 weights. A token pass removes most of the 49 paddings. Three changes clear the hard failures: the slider box, the toolbar keys and the toast timer.

Two findings change the strategy. UniFi Protect 7.2 already signs clips with SHA-256, so DashLens's evidence bundle is a lead among dashcam tools but not a new idea in video evidence. Its wording must say plainly that a local hash proves the bytes did not change after export, and that it does not prove the footage is authentic. And the per-element references point to one shared object: a selected time range that the lanes, the map, the overview strip and the export dialog all show and edit. Strava, Frigate and Resolve each have part of it. None has all of it, so this shared range is the most useful thing DashLens can build next.
